# Claim Boundary

## Allowed claims

The paper may argue, subject to a completed related-work audit, that:

- scope loss and evidence loss are important infrastructure problems for database theory;
- theorem scope should be modeled as a first-class, machine-actionable object;
- a Scoped Theorem Record should bind a claim to explicit scope axes, assumptions, complexity regime, structural restrictions, version lineage, evidence pointers, proof-technique metadata, and justified typed relations;
- the current prototype demonstrates implementation and audit feasibility on a bounded, real corpus;
- deterministic builds, hashes, validation, and source audits improve prototype reliability and reproducibility;
- substantial future research is required for extraction, verification, relation inference, version evolution, evaluation, maintenance, and governance.

## Forbidden claims

The paper must not claim or imply:

- completeness of database-theory coverage;
- completion of Corpus Tasks 4–7;
- validity of unreviewed batch 21–40 or unfinished extraction for papers 41–80;
- a final or current open-problem atlas;
- that an open problem remains open as of 2026 without a dedicated follow-up audit;
- Top50 or Top20 research directions, a primary research direction, or a completed direction funnel;
- a new database-theory theorem or complexity result;
- automatic theorem-understanding or correct autonomous extraction;
- autonomous mathematical reasoning or theorem proving;
- measured extraction accuracy, performance effectiveness, user benefit, community adoption, or coverage recall;
- scientific validation merely from tests, hashes, deterministic builds, or repository engineering;
- novelty over scholarly or mathematical knowledge systems before the related-work audit and Novelty Kill Gate;
- correctness of every extracted theoretical interpretation beyond the formally audited subset.

## Language policy

Use **machine-assisted**, **structured**, **source-grounded**, **audited**, and **human-author verified** where applicable. Avoid “automatic understanding,” “fully automated,” “correct extraction,” and equivalent language.

Use “prototype feasibility” for the audited corpus. Do not generalize the prototype into claims of effectiveness, completeness, adoption, or universal correctness.

## Fail-closed publication policy

A formal record or paper claim is promoted only when its required fields and evidence bindings satisfy the declared audit contract. Missing or ambiguous scope is represented explicitly; it is not silently inferred. Unsupported relations, open-status claims, and unreviewed extractions remain excluded from formal paper evidence.
